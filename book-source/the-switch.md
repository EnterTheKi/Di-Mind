# Prologue — Who They Are

**Di-Mind:** A bot with a globe for a head. Once lost in the noise of a 1930s black-and-white world — jazz from every radio, screaming headlines, never-ending street chatter. He wished for focus out loud, then kept walking. Until he found the switch.

**The Voice of the Radio:** The noise itself. Jazz, headlines, chatter. The Voice doesn't hate Di-Mind — it just never stops talking. When Di-Mind turns on the switch, the noise doesn't disappear. It changes. Jazz becomes rhythm. Headlines become ideas. Chatter becomes encouragement.

**The Child:** A small figure standing at the edge — the inner child. He's been there since the start. He remembers what focus felt like before everything got loud. Gentle, quiet, never demanding — just remembering.

**The Girl by the Lake:** A hidden girl near water, holding something that glows. She doesn't talk. She just holds the light. When Di-Mind reaches 9:00 and the content loop starts, her image is what fuels the vision — the forgotten memory made visible.

**The Traveler:** A guide who shows up along the way. Not a guru. A reminder. The Traveler's logbook has three steps: visualize, single-task focus, gratitude break. When Di-Mind forgets the rules, the Traveler repeats them. No new ideas — same three steps, same gold frame.

# Act I — Before the Switch

Di-Mind is lost in the noise. Every day. The Voice of the Radio plays jazz that pulls his thoughts in circles. The headlines scream things that aren't his. The street chatter asks questions he doesn't need to answer.

He tries everything. He wishes out loud — like the office boy in the old story saw the red car and said "I wish." Nothing changes. He tries to meditate but the Voice interrupts. He tries to write a list but the headlines steal his hand. He tries to be positive but the noise is louder.

2019 — the first line Di-Mind writes: "Faith. God. Holy Spirit. Ki energy — God channel." Not a wish. A signal. The tuner turned on before anything else changes.

The Child watches from the edge. He's been there since the start. He remembers what focus felt like. Not the noise-free version — the gold version. When the circuit runs clean, the noise doesn't stop. It transforms. That's the secret Di-Mind hasn't found yet.

The Girl by the Lake holds the light. She doesn't say anything. She just holds it, softly, like the inner image waiting to become the outer reality. Di-Mind hasn't looked at her yet. He's too distracted.

# The Three Rules Before They Exist

Even before Di-Mind knows the rules, he's doing pieces of them. He writes something at dawn — the first line of the list. He thinks about it — between messages, between distractions. But he talks about it too much. He tells the noise about his plans. The noise breaks the signal.

The three rules:

1. Read the list three times — morning, noon, night. Feel it. Not memorize.
2. Think of it as often as possible. Every thought is signal.
3. Don't talk about it. Not to anyone except the Ki inside. Silence protects.

The Traveler leaves these steps in Di-Mind's logbook. Not ten steps. Three. Not a course. A circuit. Read. Think. Silent. The method unfolds on its own.

Don't talk about it. Not to the Voice. Not to the group chat. Not to anyone except the Ki inside. The list is silent. The signal is protected. That's the third rule — and it's the one that breaks most circuits.
